## Measuring and Understanding Mental Health in the Context of Mass Trauma and Violence in Nigeria: From Instrument Validation to Policy Action

**ISTSS 42nd Annual Meeting · Thursday, September 24, 2026 · 1:45–3:00 PM (Central) · Salon C, Floor 3**
**Chair:** Jaimie Gradus, DSc (Boston University) · **Discussant slot:** 2:45–3:00 PM

### Why this symposium

Nigeria, the most populous country in Africa, faces multiple overlapping violent conflicts, and screening at scale is task-shifted to lay workers using instruments whose measurement properties were largely unknown in this population. The symposium follows one research program (MaRVIN) across that whole arc: validating the instruments against psychiatrist interview, deploying them across the six geopolitical zones, probing what the results say about how distress is structured, and handing the findings to the Federal Ministry of Health under Nigeria's 2021 Mental Health Act.

### The four papers

**1. Population-Specific Validation of PCL-5, PHQ-9, and GAD-7 Among Violence-Exposed Persons in Nigeria: Why Local Calibration Cannot Be Skipped**
*Jaimie Gradus (Boston University) · 1:49–2:03 PM*
Validation of the three screens among 205 adults in two IDP camps in Abuja, translated into Hausa and Pidgin English, with psychiatrist-administered structured clinical interviews as the criterion standard. Reliability was excellent (McDonald's ω = .92, .91, .87), confirmatory factor analysis favored a six-factor anhedonia model for the PCL-5, and the population-specific cut-points differ from the conventional ones, which is the paper's central warning.

**2. Prevalence and Predictors of PTSD, Depression, and Anxiety Among Persons Exposed to Diverse Forms of Armed Violence Across Six Nigerian States: A Cross-Zonal Epidemiological Study**
*Salma Abdalla (Washington University in St. Louis) · 2:03–2:17 PM*
The instruments deployed at scale: 1,774 conflict-affected adults across states in all six geopolitical zones, spanning distinct forms of armed violence. Survey-adjusted prevalence: PTSD 18.5%, depression 21.0%, anxiety 20.7%; nearly one in three met criteria for at least one condition, with 28-fold variation across states. Each additional traumatic event carried 14-15% higher prevalence of every condition, and current IDP camp residence carried 1.9- to 2.9-fold higher prevalence after adjustment for trauma, with exceptional community-level clustering (ICCs 0.49-0.86).

**3. When More Trauma Means Less Stress Sensitivity: Evidence for a Trauma Saturation Effect Among Violence-Exposed Populations in Nigeria**
*Mohammed Abba-Aji (Washington University in St. Louis) · 2:17–2:31 PM*
Using the six-state MaRVIN sample, this paper shows that the association between socioeconomic stressors and mental health is concentrated among adults with little or no trauma exposure: the per-stressor PTSD prevalence ratio is 1.97 among the unexposed and null among those with five or more event types, with the interaction below 0.0001 across PTSD, depression, and anxiety. Trauma was modeled continuously with restricted cubic splines, and the pattern held on the absolute scale and across alternative thresholds and continuous scores, challenging simple dose-response assumptions and arguing for matching psychosocial interventions to trauma history.

**4. From Evidence to Action: Translating MaRVIN Study Findings Into Policy Implementation Under Nigeria's 2021 Mental Health Act**
*Tunde MasseyFerguson Ojo (Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare, Nigeria) · 2:31–2:45 PM*

### Threads a discussant might pull

These are live tensions inside the data, and the team is prepared to engage any of them:

- Local calibration versus cross-country comparability: population-specific cut-points improve clinical accuracy and complicate global burden estimates at the same time.
- At scale, depression and anxiety scores were statistically close to inseparable (latent r = .94), with a dominant general distress dimension. What should condition-specific screening batteries look like if distress is mostly one dimension in this population?
- Task-sharing implications: a 36-item three-instrument battery, administered by lay workers, against Nigeria's mental health workforce shortage.
- What a working measurement-to-policy pipeline looks like when a ministry co-owns the research question, and what other conflict-affected settings can copy from it.
